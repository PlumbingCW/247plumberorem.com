How coverage works for emergencies.

Emergency calls are answered 24 hours a day. Dispatch asks for your address first, then matches the job with a licensed and insured plumber on duty. Travel from Orem to the neighboring towns is short, but timing depends on traffic and how many calls are active, so dispatch will not promise an arrival time.

Water, sewer and storm drainage are run by each city, so the point where the public line ends and your private line begins can differ from town to town. The plumber can tell you which side of the line a problem sits on, and your city can confirm the boundary.

A note on arrival timing.

Customers often ask how fast a plumber can reach a town. Honest answers depend on who is on duty, how many urgent calls are active and what traffic looks like on State Street, University Parkway and I-15 at that hour.

Dispatch will tell you what it can on the call. For a flooding emergency, the fastest thing you can do is shut off the main water valve before help arrives, which stops the damage while you wait.

If you do not know where your main valve is, ask dispatch to talk you through finding it. It is usually where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, utility room or garage, and a minute spent locating it now saves an hour of cleanup later.

What stays the same across the whole area.

Utah County sits in a valley with cold winters, so freeze damage, shallow groundwater and aging supply lines show up in every town. Orem's design criteria list a 30 inch frost depth, and nearby cities set their own standards, so confirm yours.

Utah Geological Survey guidance notes that shallow groundwater can flood basements and damage buried lines, and that sump pumps should be checked each winter. That advice applies across Utah Valley, which is why sump pump and drain calls rise after wet seasons.
