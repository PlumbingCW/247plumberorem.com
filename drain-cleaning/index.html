Most drain calls come down to one question: is this a single clogged pipe or a symptom of something deeper? The visit is built to answer that before anyone starts cutting or replacing parts.

Drain backing up right now: what to do first.

Water coming up through a drain means the line is blocked below that fixture, and every gallon you add has nowhere to go. Stop using that sink, tub or shower. Do not run the dishwasher or washing machine, because both pump out a lot of water in a short burst.

If the backup is in a basement floor drain or the lowest tub, treat it as a main line warning and tell the dispatcher so. Do not pour a caustic drain opener into standing water. It sits on the clog, can burn skin when you plunge, and makes the technician's job riskier.

Wet electrical outlets, power strips and appliance cords near the water are the real hazard. Keep people away from them. Shut power off at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Clogs do not follow business hours. A blocked kitchen drain on a holiday weekend or a tub that backs up at night can be dispatched the same day when an appointment is available, and the phone is answered around the clock.

Kitchen sink clogs, and why bathroom drains fail differently.

Kitchen drains clog with grease, food starch and coffee grounds. Warm grease runs down as a liquid, cools inside the trap arm and the horizontal run behind the wall, and coats the pipe like candle wax. Rice, pasta and potato peelings stick to that film, and over time a 1.5 inch line can narrow considerably. That is why a kitchen clog often returns right after a plunger seems to fix it.

Bathroom sink and tub drains clog with hair, soap scum and toothpaste. Hair wraps around the pop-up stopper or the lower strainer and knits into a mat. Shower and tub drains are usually two inches across, so they clear well with a small cable, but the mat is often sitting just past the strainer where a hand tool can reach it.

Laundry standpipes and utility sinks fill with lint and detergent residue. A floor drain in a garage or basement collects sediment and, if it connects to the main line, may back up when the main is slow.

A drain line leak is a different matter. If the cabinet under the sink is wet only when water is draining, or a ceiling stain appears below a bathroom, the problem may be a cracked or loose drain pipe instead of a clog. A cable cannot fix that, and the technician will check joints and the trap arm before running a machine.

What happens on a drain cleaning visit.

The sequence below is how a typical call goes. The tools change with the clog, but the order does not.

  1. Find where the blockage starts

    The plumber runs water at several fixtures to see which ones drain and which gurgle. One slow fixture means a branch clog. Several slow fixtures means the shared line.

  2. Give you the price before work

    Once the scope is clear, you hear the cost in plain terms before any cabling starts, including what happens if the first method does not open the line.

  3. Open the line

    A hand auger handles trap-level clogs. A drum cable machine with a cutting head handles clogs farther back. For grease, a water jet may be the better choice.

  4. Test and clean up

    The plumber flushes the line with a few gallons of hot water and watches for slow spots or leaks at the joints. Drop cloths protect the floor and the removed debris is bagged.

  5. Explain what caused it

    If hair, grease or a structural fault caused the clog, you get a straight answer and a way to prevent a repeat, including when a camera look makes sense.

Before you call: what to check and write down.

Two minutes of notes help the dispatcher send the right gear and give you a more accurate price.

  • Which fixtures are slow or backing up, and whether the problem is one drain or several.
  • When it started, and whether it followed heavy water use, a load of laundry or a storm.
  • Whether you used a chemical drain opener, and which one. The plumber needs to know before handling the water.
  • Whether a toilet gurgles or a basement floor drain bubbles when another fixture drains.
  • Where the house cleanout is, if you know. It is usually a capped pipe in the yard, garage, basement or crawlspace.
  • Anything dropped into the drain, such as a toy, a ring or a stopper.
  • A photo of any water damage and the date, in case you need it for an insurance claim.

What drain cleaning costs and how to read the quote.

The total depends on a few things you can see ahead of time. Every job is quoted before work starts, so you are never guessing.

Where the clog sits

A clog at a tub strainer is a short job. A clog 50 feet down a branch line takes a larger machine and more time.

Access to the pipe

A working cleanout makes the line easy to reach. If the plumber has to pull a toilet or remove a trap to get in, the labor grows.

Tool required

A hand auger, a cable machine and a water jet run on different equipment, and the price follows the tool the clog needs.

Time of the call

Calls at night, on weekends and on holidays are billed differently from daytime appointments, and you should hear the difference up front.

Repeat or underlying cause

A clog caused by a broken pipe or roots means the cleaning is only part of the repair, and the quote should say so.

Homeowners policies typically cover sudden and accidental water damage and typically do not cover wear, gradual leaks or groundwater flooding. A plain clog is usually a maintenance cost, not a claim. Check your own policy if a backup has damaged floors or belongings.
