What to have ready when you call about a commercial property.

A short, clear description saves the first fifteen minutes of any dispatch call.

  • The street address and the suite or unit number, plus whether you own the building or lease the space.
  • Where the main water shutoff is and whether you have already closed it.
  • What is affected: a single restroom, a kitchen line, a floor drain, a roof drain or an entire building.
  • Whether the water is clean supply water, gray water or sewage, since sewage changes the cleanup plan.
  • Whether the building has a grease interceptor, a backflow assembly or a lift pump, and when each was last serviced.
  • Property management contact details and any work order number that your landlord requires.

After-hours commercial emergencies: what to stop right now.

A pipe failure at 11 p.m. in a closed shop floods quietly for hours. Shut the main or the zone valve first, then turn off the water heater supply if the hot side is leaking, and lift or cover anything stored on the floor. Do not step into standing water near panels, outlets or equipment.

Sewage in a floor drain or restroom is a different category. Close the restroom, stop using the fixtures upstream and call right away, because contaminated water can shut a food business until it is cleaned and cleared.

Nights, weekends and holidays get the same 24/7 dispatch, and same-day appointments when available cover issues that are annoying but not active, such as a slow drain or a dripping valve in a back-of-house sink.

Tracing a leak in a commercial building.

Commercial leak detection is harder than residential because lines run through ceilings, shared walls, mechanical rooms and under slab. Crews use acoustic listening equipment, thermal imaging, tracer gas and pipe locators, and the method depends on the line material and the building. Ask which method the technician will bring when you call.

A leak call can end without a repair when the water traced back to a neighboring suite, a roof drain, a condensate line or a landlord-owned riser. Have a facility map, previous water bills and the meter reading ready, because a flat reading when everything is shut down is the first useful clue.

Where the leak sits in a shared line, the repair may belong to the landlord or the building owner, and the plumber can document where it is. For a deeper look at methods, see how leak detection works in homes and buildings.

Backflow testing, grease traps and drains.

A backflow assembly keeps used water from drifting back into the supply. Testing is typically required on a regular schedule by the water supplier under Utah cross connection rules, so ask the Orem Water Division which assemblies on your property need it, and keep the test report on file. This is planned work, not a rescue call.

Grease interceptors fill up with fats and food solids and, when neglected, push grease into the building line. Cleaning is a pumping job, and a clogged outlet sends the problem downstream. Floor drains, trench drains and storm drains need periodic flushing to prevent overflow during a heavy rain.

A recurring backup in a mainline points to the line itself, which is when cleaning the sewer line to the main with a cable or jet matters more than another plunger. A lift pump that fails in a basement restroom is repaired or replaced, depending on the float, check valve and motor.
