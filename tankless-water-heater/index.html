A tankless water heater in Orem, UT is a wall-mounted unit that heats water only while a tap is open, using a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger instead of a stored tank.

When it runs well, it does not run out of stored hot water, though every unit has a flow limit and a limit on how much it can raise the water temperature. When it needs attention, the signs are subtle: a little less heat, a flicker of an error code, a sound the unit did not make last year.

This page is for homeowners who already own one and wonder who services it, and for those weighing one for a new install. Licensed and insured plumbers service these units, and every visit begins with a phone call.

My on-demand heater needs service: what that usually means.

People describe the same few problems. The water is warm but not hot. The unit shuts off partway through a shower. A code flashes on the display. Or the heater has not been touched since install and someone mentioned cleaning it. All of these point to tankless water heater service: descaling the heat exchanger, cleaning filters, checking vents and testing sensors.

Cold winter inlet water also lowers how much hot water a tankless unit can deliver at once, so a unit that keeps up in summer may struggle in January. Condensing models add a condensate drain that needs periodic checking.

The heat exchanger is the narrow passage where the water picks up heat, and minerals settle there as scale. A thin layer slows heat transfer, so the unit works harder and trips its own safety limits. That is why the service schedule matters more on tankless units than on a tank, where the same minerals mostly collect on the bottom.

If your heater has stopped working altogether, or you smell gas, the situation is different. Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first for gas, and call us for a unit that has simply stopped heating.

Signs your tankless heater is due for a flush.

These are the usual early signs, in roughly the order they appear.

  • Hot water that takes longer to reach the tap or never gets quite as hot as it once did.
  • A flame or heating cycle that starts and stops during one shower.
  • Rattling, kettle-like sounds from inside the unit when it fires.
  • Reduced flow at hot taps, which can mean a blocked inlet screen or scaled passages.
  • An error code that comes back after a reset.
  • It has been a year or more since the last flush, or nobody knows when it was last done.

Orem water and why tankless units collect scale.

The City of Orem's 2022 water conservation plan describes three kinds of source: deep wells, city springs and Provo River water treated at the regional plant. The share of each shifts over the year, and water chemistry can differ by season and by where a home sits in the city. A tankless unit sees that chemistry in a very direct way, because the heat exchanger is where the minerals drop out.

We do not publish a hardness figure for Orem, and neither should anyone else guess one for your house. Read the utility's annual water quality report and do a simple tap test, such as watching for white crust on a kettle or a showerhead. The result tells you whether to flush yearly, more often, or whether to look at a water softener or filter upstream of the heater.

Many Orem houses were built between 1950 and 1980, so the gas line and venting serving a tankless unit may be older than the unit. Before upgrading, ask the plumber to check the gas pipe size and the vent route, because tankless burners draw more gas at once than a typical tank.

What to do before the installer arrives for a tankless unit.

A tankless install touches gas, venting and electrical systems, so a little preparation is also a safety step.

  • If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call anyone else.
  • Clear a path and a working area around the old heater and the planned wall location.
  • Find your gas meter size and the main electrical panel, and note any spare breaker spaces for the installer.
  • Do not relight pilot lights or adjust gas valves on a unit you plan to replace.
  • Know where the main water shutoff is, because the install requires turning off water to the whole house.
  • Ask the installer which permits apply, since permit rules vary by job and the Orem Building Safety Division can confirm.

How a tankless flush and descale is done.

A flush starts with turning off the gas or power and closing the water isolation valves. The tech connects a small pump and two hoses to the service ports, then circulates a mild descaling solution, often white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved cleaner, through the heat exchanger for roughly 45 minutes to an hour. Clean water is then run through to rinse everything out.

The inlet screen is pulled and cleaned, the vent is inspected for blockages, and the unit is fired up to check temperature and flow. A clean flush will not repair a failed part, but it often restores lost heat. Units installed without service valves need those added first, which is why a tech may suggest adding them.

Ask when you call which method the technician will bring, whether the unit has isolation valves, and what brand and model it is. The answers decide how long the visit takes. If the unit is beyond cleaning, water heater repair covers diagnosing worn parts, and water heater installation explains replacing or converting.

What affects the cost of tankless service.

Service on a wall unit is usually predictable once the model is known. Here is what moves the number.

Service valves

Units with isolation valves flush quickly. Units without them need valves installed first.

Brand and model

Parts and diagnostics vary by manufacturer, and some boards or sensors are costly.

Scale load

A unit that has gone years without a flush needs a longer circulation time.

Gas or electric type

Gas units add vent and burner checks. Electric units add element and circuit checks.

Install work, if needed

A new tankless install adds gas line sizing, venting, and sometimes electrical work.

Routine maintenance and age-related failure fall outside insurance. If a leak from the unit has damaged the house, read your own policy, since sudden and accidental losses are the kind typically covered.

Limits and honest trade-offs.

Tankless is not always the right answer. The units cost more up front, need more gas or electric capacity, and need regular service in areas where minerals build up. Electric tankless models draw a very large load, so a licensed electrician should check the panel before one is installed.

This page does not cover brand-specific warranty rules, and we do not claim factory certification for any brand. If a repair would void a warranty, check the paperwork first. Whole-home changes belong in a planning call, not a quick fix.
