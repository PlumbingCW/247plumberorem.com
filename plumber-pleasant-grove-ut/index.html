Winter in Pleasant Grove: when a frozen line becomes an emergency.

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for its Pleasant Grove station list a January average low of about 24 degrees Fahrenheit and 11.5 inches of snow, with December close behind at 10.3 inches. Those are averages, so single nights run colder, and a supply line in an unheated crawl space, garage wall or north-facing exterior wall can freeze on one of them. That is the first line to suspect when one faucet suddenly delivers nothing.

A frozen pipe is a warning, not yet a flood. The flood arrives when the ice thaws and a split in the pipe opens. If you hear hissing, see water staining a ceiling or find a wet wall, close the main valve and call for emergency burst pipe repair. The plumber cuts out the split section, replaces it with new pipe and checks the neighboring runs for damage from the same freeze.

Winter also loads water heaters. Incoming water is at its coldest, the tank runs longer, and a unit that has been weeping at the base since summer often quits on the first deep-freeze morning. A puddle under the tank, rust-colored hot water or no hot water at all is a job for water heater repair, or replacement when the tank itself has corroded through.

Frozen pipe or burst pipe: what to do first.

Find the shutoff before you need it. Pleasant Grove's public works FAQ notes that most houses have a valve just inside the house where the water line enters. The city asks residents not to operate the meter except in a real emergency, and tampering with the meter or meter box is against the law, so use the house valve first.

Open the faucet the frozen line feeds so melting water has somewhere to go, and keep the heat on in the cold space. Do not use an open flame to thaw a pipe. If water has reached outlets, light fixtures or the panel, keep people away from wet electrical areas, and shut power off at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Then call (385) 488-1892, day or night.

Spring startup: April pressure finds the pipes that froze.

Pleasant Grove runs a secondary water system for irrigation that is separate from drinking water. The city's secondary water page says it starts filling the system around mid-April, asks residents to wait until May 1 to water, and tells them to open their valve slowly to check for leaks. It adds that a broken pipe was most likely frozen over the winter. A slow open means you spot a fountain in a flowerbed before it runs all night.

Irrigation piping past the secondary valve is sprinkler work, but the house supply line, hose bibs and everything on the drinking water side are plumbing. If you cannot tell which system a wet patch belongs to, shut off every fixture and watch the leak indicator on the culinary meter. A moving indicator means the loss is on the drinking water side, which is where leak detection with acoustic listening gear, thermal imaging or pipe locators comes in.

The city also warns that secondary water is not for drinking and that drinking or playing in it can make people sick. Keep it away from anything drinkable.

Plumbing repairs after a freeze follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) edition the city has adopted. Confirm the edition and any permit with the Pleasant Grove Building Division.

Summer: strained pressure, hidden leaks and roots in the sewer lateral.

When pressure drops inside the house, the city's public works FAQ says the cause is often a pressure regulator in the home going bad, and advises having a plumber check it. Since a weak regulator also stresses fixtures and the water heater, it is worth testing before the next failure.

The city's own November 2025 draft water element for the general plan lists unmetered water loss from leaks as a strain on the culinary system. A hidden leak at your house is a small piece of the same problem, and it shows up as a bill that climbs while habits stay the same.

Summer is also when slow drains turn into backups, because roots push into joints in older laterals while the ground is dry. Per the city FAQ, the owner maintains the lateral that serves the home and the city checks the main. A backup in the lowest fixture calls for sewer line cleaning of your own line, plus a call to the city if the main is involved.

October drain-down: how Pleasant Grove homes avoid the January call.

The city's secondary water page gave October 15 as the 2026 shutdown date. It says the system drains for about a week afterward, with water still present in some pipe networks for 5 to 8 days. It asks residents to leave their own valve open until Thanksgiving so no water is trapped, drain the system fully, and leave the filter on. Do not touch the city's valve. A shutoff of your own belongs after it, outside the city box.

On the drinking water side, the fall job is smaller: disconnect and store hoses, close and drain exterior spigots, and look for any line that runs through an unheated space. These steps cost little compared with a split pipe in January, and they are the reason winter emergency calls are usually about the lines nobody remembered.

Before we arrive in Pleasant Grove: four things to do.

A few minutes of preparation limits damage and helps the plumber start work faster.

Close the house main

The valve is usually just inside the house where the line enters. Turn it off for any leak you cannot stop at a fixture.

Photograph the damage

Take dated photos before cleanup. Sudden, accidental damage is what homeowners policies usually cover, while slow leaks, wear and groundwater flooding usually are not, so read your own policy.

Clear the way

Move boxes from the water heater, crawl space hatch, cleanout and under-sink cabinets so the plumber can reach them.

Note what you see

Write down which fixtures are affected, whether the water is clear, brown or sewage, and when it started. Have the street address ready for the call.

Pleasant Grove fall and winter checklist.

Work through this before the first hard freeze, then again before leaving town for the holidays.

  • Know where the house main valve is and confirm it turns without force.
  • Disconnect hoses and drain exterior spigots after the secondary system shuts down.
  • Check crawl spaces, garages and north-facing walls for exposed supply lines.
  • Look at the base of the water heater for rust streaks or moisture.
  • Leave heat on in the house, even when traveling, so interior walls stay above freezing.
