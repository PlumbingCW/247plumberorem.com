Water Heater Hose Replacement in Orem, UT: What to Check and When to Call

The two flexible lines on top of your tank are small parts with a big leak risk. Here is how to judge them.

By the Orem 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 23, 2026

Call (385) 488-1892See all services

A water heater hose is the short flexible connector that links your shutoff valves to the tank. These connectors are often the first thing to fail on an otherwise healthy heater, and a slow weep at the crimp can soak a garage floor or closet for days before anyone notices.

If a hose is spraying or the floor around the tank is wet right now, close the cold water shutoff valve on the line above the heater and call right away. Dispatch runs 24/7. Keep people and pets out of the wet area, and leave the gas controls of a gas heater alone.

What the two hoses actually do.

Every storage tank has a cold inlet and a hot outlet on top. Between each port and the house piping sits a flexible connector, usually a braided stainless sleeve over a rubber or plastic liner, with a threaded nut on each end. The flex lets the tank be set down slightly off line with the pipes and lets it move a little as it heats and cools.

A gas heater has a third line, the gas connector, which is a different part with different rules. Treat that one as plumber-only work. A hose swap on the water side is a separate job and does not involve the gas control valve at all.

Signs a hose is near the end of its life.

Most failures give a warning. Look with a flashlight and a dry paper towel, because a seep that evaporates leaves only a faint ring.

  • White or green crust on the nut or the crimped ferrule, which means water has been escaping slowly.
  • Rust-colored stains or broken wire strands in the braided sleeve.
  • A hose that is kinked, stretched tight, or bent sharply right at the fitting.
  • A damp paper towel after you wipe the underside of each connection and wait ten minutes.
  • Water stains on the floor or on the tank jacket directly below the inlet and outlet.
  • Hoses that were installed with the heater and have never been looked at since.

What you see and what it usually means.

Use this as a quick sort before you pick up a wrench. It is a decision aid, not a diagnosis, because a leak at the top of the tank can come from more than one part.

Drip at the nut where the hose meets the pipeLoose or worn fitting, or a failing washer inside the nutShut the valve, check the washer, and replace the hose if the seat is damaged.
Weeping at the crimped end of the braidHose body failing at the ferruleReplace the hose, never tighten the nut harder to stop it.

How a hose swap works on an electric or gas storage tank.

This is the general sequence for a straightforward replacement on a tank with working shutoff valves. Skip it if the valves do not close fully or the fittings are corroded.

  1. Cut the energy

    Turn an electric heater off at its breaker, or set a gas heater control to pilot or off. Never leave an electric element powered with the tank drained.

  2. Close the cold supply and relieve pressure

    Shut the cold valve, then open a hot faucet in the house so the tank can take in air and the lines stop pushing.

  3. Unscrew and set the old hoses aside

    Have a bucket under each connection. A little water will come out of the connector itself.

  4. Fit new connectors

    Use new rated connectors of the same thread size and a length that makes a gentle curve, not a tight bend. Replace the washers instead of reusing them.

  5. Refill and check

    Open the cold valve with the hot faucet still open until it runs steady, then close the faucet and check each joint with a dry towel for several minutes.

Choosing the right replacement connector.

Match the connector to the job in three ways: the thread size at each end, the length, and the rating on the label. Take the old hose to the store or photograph the stamped markings. A connector rated for potable water and for hot water is the only kind that belongs here, so check that the label says potable and hot water rated, and do not substitute a washing machine hose.

Many installers also fit dielectric unions or nipples where copper meets the steel tank port. Dissimilar metals corrode at the joint, and that corrosion is a common reason a fresh hose starts weeping within a season. If your tank ports already look chalky, price the work as a heater repair visit instead of a ten-minute swap. When a hose job turns into a heater decision.

A hose problem sometimes exposes a bigger one. If the shutoff valve will not close, the pipe behind it is corroded, or the leak traces back to the tank body, the cheap fix is gone. Tank walls cannot be patched, and a tank that is leaking from its shell is finished.

That is the point to compare a water heater repair visit against a full replacement and ask the plumber which one the symptoms support. Bring up the tank's age and where the water first appeared. A hose that failed on a young tank is just a hose, and an old tank with a rusted port is a different conversation.

What drives the cost of a hose replacement.

The part itself is a small line item. The total changes with whether the shutoff valves still work, whether the ports need new dielectric fittings, how hard the tank is to reach, and whether the visit happens after hours. A planned weekday swap and a night call for a spraying connector are billed differently, and a clear price before work starts is the best way to compare them.

When you compare two quotes, ask each plumber to separate parts, labor, any trip or after-hours charge, and anything they would do beyond the hose. If one quote quietly includes a new shutoff valve and the other does not, they are not the same job.
