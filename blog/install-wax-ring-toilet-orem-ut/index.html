Installing a Wax Ring for a Toilet: A Homeowner Guide for Orem, UT

A wax ring seals the toilet to the drain. When it fails, the fix is simple, but the details matter.

By the Orem 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 23, 2026

Call (385) 488-1892See all services

The wax ring is a thick donut of wax that sits between the bottom of the toilet and the flange on the floor. It seals the drain opening so water and sewer gas stay inside the pipe. When it fails, you may see water around the base after a flush, feel the bowl rock, or smell sewer gas in the bathroom.

Replacing one is a job many homeowners can do in an afternoon. This guide covers the tools, the steps, what to inspect before you set the toilet back, and the cases where it is better to stop and call a plumber.

Signs the wax ring has failed.

Any of these points toward the seal, though a leaking supply line or tank bolt can mimic them.

  • Water on the floor around the base that appears after flushing, not just when the tank refills.
  • A toilet that rocks when you sit or push on it.
  • A sewer smell in the bathroom with no visible backup.
  • A stained or soft spot in the floor beside the toilet.
  • Water that disappears from under the toilet when you dry it, then returns after a flush.

Tools and parts to gather.

You need a new wax ring (a version with a plastic horn is optional), new closet bolts if the old ones are corroded, an adjustable wrench, a putty knife, rags, a bucket, a sponge and a pair of rubber gloves. A second person helps, because a full toilet is heavy and awkward to lift. Have a flange repair ring on hand in case the flange turns out to be damaged.

Match the ring to your toilet. Some are made for standard drains and others for thicker floors or low-flow bowls. Check the package, and if you are unsure which fits, ask at the store with your toilet's model number in hand.

Step by step: pulling and resetting the toilet.

Work slowly. A rushed reset is the most common reason a new ring leaks.

  1. Shut off and drain

    Close the supply valve at the wall, flush, hold the handle down, and sponge out the remaining water from the tank and bowl.

  2. Disconnect and lift

    Undo the supply line and the nuts on the closet bolts, then rock the toilet gently to break the seal and lift it straight up. Set it on towels or cardboard.

  3. Scrape and inspect

    Remove the old wax from the flange and the toilet horn with a putty knife. Look at the flange for cracks, rust, or a bent ring, and check that it sits level with the finished floor.

  4. Set the new ring

    Place the ring on the flange or the toilet horn, as the package directs, and set the toilet straight down over the bolts in one motion. Do not twist it or slide it sideways.

  5. Press, tighten, test

    Press down evenly, then snug the nuts alternating from side to side. Over-tightening can crack the porcelain, so stop as soon as the bowl no longer rocks and check it for movement again after a day of use. Reconnect the supply, refill the tank, flush several times and look for water at the base.

What the flange tells you.

The flange is the ring bolted to the floor around the drain. If it sits well below the finished floor, a standard ring may not compress enough to seal, and a thicker ring or a flange extender is usually the remedy. If it sits above the floor, it can stop the toilet from seating flat.

A cracked or corroded flange, a loose one, or a floor soft from long-term leaks is a repair job, not a ring job. The toilet will keep leaking until the base it sits on is sound. This is the point where toilet repair by a licensed plumber is the safer choice.

New toilets, old floors and who to call.

If the toilet is cracked or you want to change the bowl, a new install includes a fresh ring, bolts and a supply line. The plumbing installation page describes how fixture work is scoped, including rough-in checks and permit questions.

A wax ring is also worth replacing every time a toilet comes off the floor. Do not reuse the old one, even if it looks fine. Wax that has been compressed does not recover its shape well.
