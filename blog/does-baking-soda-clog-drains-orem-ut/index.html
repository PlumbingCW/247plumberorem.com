Does Baking Soda Clog Drains? What Orem, UT Homes Need to Know

The short answer is that baking soda is harmless and weak. The longer answer decides whether you waste an afternoon.

By the Orem 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 23, 2026

Call (385) 488-1892See all services

Plain baking soda does not clog drains on its own. It dissolves in water, so a spoonful poured down a sink will not form a plug. The trouble comes from expecting it to clear a blockage it cannot touch, and from the habits that go along with it, such as pouring chemicals on top of a standing clog.

This guide explains what baking soda and vinegar do inside a pipe, which clogs respond to simple methods and which do not, and how to tell when a slow drain in Orem, UT has moved past a home fix. Everything here is general and carries no dollar figures.

What baking soda and vinegar actually do in a pipe.

Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, and vinegar is dilute acetic acid. When they meet, they react to form carbon dioxide gas, water and a salt, which is where the fizz comes from. The reaction is short and mild, and most of the foam rises out of the drain instead of pushing down into it.

That has two practical consequences. First, the fizz does not mechanically dislodge a solid plug of hair or a grease mass, because the bubbles carry almost no force. Second, the mixture is gentle on pipes, which is why it is a reasonable way to freshen a drain with a faint odor. It is a cleaner for film and smell, and a poor tool for a stoppage.

Why the fizz method fails on a real blockage.

A standing clog needs one of two things: to be broken apart, or to be pulled out. A hand auger or a plunger does that physically. A gas bubble in a vinegar mixture does not, and the liquid mostly sits above the clog or flows around it.

Some homeowners follow the fizz with a kettle of boiling water. Boiling water can soften grease, but it can also stress some fixtures and soften plastic pipe such as PVC, and it does nothing for a hair mass. Hot tap water is the safer choice for a kitchen line, and a plumber can tell you what your particular pipes will tolerate.

If you want something stronger than a plunger or hand tool for a greasy or organic film, an enzyme or bacterial drain product is the milder option. It works slowly on film and does nothing for a solid plug, so read the label and never combine it with another product.

Never mix drain chemicals, and stop if one has already been used.

If you have already poured a commercial drain cleaner into a clogged drain, do not add baking soda, vinegar or another product on top. Mixing household chemicals can release hazardous fumes, and a drain full of caustic liquid is dangerous for the person who opens the trap afterwards. Tell the plumber what you used and when, so they can protect themselves.

Keep the bathroom ventilated and keep children and pets away until the plumber has seen it.

What to try before you call, in a sensible order.

These steps are safe for most homes and take little time.

  • Check whether the problem is one fixture or several. Run a nearby sink and a tub and listen for gurgling.
  • Remove the stopper or strainer and clear visible hair and debris by hand with a glove.
  • Use a plunger sized for the fixture, with enough water to cover the cup, and give it a dozen firm strokes.
  • Place a bucket under the sink trap, unscrew it and clear the packed material, then refit the washers.
  • Run hot tap water for a minute and check the flow. If it is still slow, stop.
  • Avoid chemical cleaners, since they often leave a caustic pool that makes later work riskier.
  • Write down which fixtures were slow, what you tried, and when the problem began, so the plumber starts with more than a guess.

Signs that the problem is in the main line.

A sewer line problem shows up in the lowest fixtures first. A basement floor drain that bubbles, a toilet that gurgles when the washer drains, or water in the tub after a flush all point past the fixtures. Home methods cannot reach that line, and repeated chemicals only delay the fix.

If that sounds familiar, sewer line cleaning is the right service to ask about. A technician can run a cable or a high-pressure water jet through the main line and, if the blockage keeps coming back, follow it with a camera pass. When a drain needs professional cleaning.

Call for drain cleaning when a plunger and a trap cleaning have not helped, when the clog returns within weeks, or when more than one fixture is affected. A professional cable reaches past the trap and through the bends that a hand tool cannot clear, and water jetting scours the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the plug.
