What Drives the Cost of a 40 Gallon Water Heater Installation in Orem, UT

The tank is only one line on the quote. Here is what else moves the total, and how to compare two bids fairly.

By the Orem 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 23, 2026

Call (385) 488-1892See all services

A 40 gallon tank is the size most households ask about first, and the price question usually comes right behind it. The honest answer is that two installs of the same tank can land far apart, because the tank is only one piece of the job. Venting, gas or electric connections, code upgrades, permits and old-tank disposal each add or remove work.

This guide walks through the line items on a typical quote, shows where the totals diverge, and gives you a method for comparing two bids without guessing. It does not quote dollar figures. Any number printed on a web page cannot know your vent run, your shutoff valves or your access, so a real price needs a phone conversation about your actual setup.

What a quote for a 40 gallon tank is made of.

Most quotes break into five pieces: the heater itself, materials to connect it, labor, permit and inspection handling, and removal of the old unit. The heater varies by fuel type, efficiency rating, warranty length and brand tier. Materials include new flexible supply connectors, a temperature and pressure relief valve discharge line, gas flex connector or electrical whip, and sometimes a drain pan.

Labor depends on how much of that is already in good shape. A like-for-like swap of a gas tank in the same closet with a sound vent is the short end of the range. Moving the heater, re-sizing the vent, or adding a drain pan with a drain line makes the job longer.

Where two quotes for the same tank diverge.

When one bid is noticeably higher, the gap almost always sits in one of these rows. Ask each plumber to state the line in writing before you compare totals.

Orem, UT: water source and permit facts that affect your install

Per the city's 2022 water conservation plan, Orem draws culinary water from nine deep wells, two city-owned springs, and treated Provo River water. With three kinds of source feeding the system, water chemistry can differ by season and by where a home sits, and mineral scale can build inside a heated tank. The practical step is to read the city's annual water quality report and ask your plumber about tank flushing and the sacrificial anode rod at each service.

Orem's adopted codes list names the 2021 IPC with Utah amendments, and the city's permit page says anyone who causes plumbing work to be done needs a permit, with listed exceptions. That means you should ask the Orem Building Safety Division about your specific swap before the work date rather than assume either way.

Gas versus electric changes more than the fuel bill.

A gas 40 gallon heater needs a vent path to the outdoors, a gas shutoff, a sediment trap on the gas line, and enough combustion air in the room. An electric one needs no vent, but it does need a dedicated circuit of the right size, and a licensed electrician should confirm the panel can carry it if anything changes. Read the nameplate on the unit you are replacing and give the plumber that information when you call.

Swapping fuel types is a bigger project than swapping tanks. Moving from gas to electric or the other way adds new connections and sometimes new code questions. Ask for those items priced separately so you can see what the conversion itself costs.

A tank is not always the right replacement.

If the tank is leaking from the body, no part swap will save it, and the conversation moves to replacing the water heater rather than repairing it. If the problem is a failed element, thermostat or burner part on a younger tank, repair may be the smarter spend. The plumber should tell you which side of that line your unit is on and why.

Size matters too. Forty gallons suits many households of three or four people, but a house with a large soaking tub or back-to-back showers may run short. The first-hour rating, which appears on the manufacturer's spec sheet and usually on the EnergyGuide label, tells you how much hot water the unit can deliver in a busy hour, and it is worth comparing rather than looking at tank volume alone.

How to compare two quotes line by line.

Put the bids side by side and check these points before you look at the totals.

  • Same fuel type, same tank size, and the same warranty length on both.
  • Vent work listed as reused, repaired, or replaced, not left unstated.
  • Permit and inspection named as included or excluded.
  • New shutoff valves, connectors and relief valve discharge line listed.
  • Old tank removal and disposal included or priced as an add-on.
  • A clear statement of the price before work starts, with any possible add-ons explained in advance.
  • The plumber's Utah license class checked on the state license lookup.

Orem's published codes list the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with current Utah amendments, and the city's Building Safety Division handles permits. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Division whether your swap needs one, and ask your plumber who pulls it.
