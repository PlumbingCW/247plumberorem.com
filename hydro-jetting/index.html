If a main or branch line is blocked and water is rising in a tub or floor drain, that is urgent, and Orem dispatch runs 24/7. Until the plumber arrives, stop running water, keep laundry and dishwasher cycles off, and keep anyone away from wet outlets near the backed-up fixture.

Hydro jetting in Orem, UT is the cleaning of a drain or sewer line with a nozzle that sprays water at very high pressure against the inside of the pipe. The water cuts through grease, sludge, mineral scale and fine roots, then flushes the debris down the line. It is different from cabling, which punches a hole through a clog and leaves the rest of the wall as it was.

The snake only made a small hole, and the clog came back.

This is the most common reason people look for jetting. A cable went in, water ran for a few days, and then the same fixture slowed down again. Or the cable hung up at a bend and the technician could not get past it. In both cases the clog was a thick coating, not a single plug.

A cable cutting head clears a channel about as wide as the head. A grease or scale coat on the rest of the wall stays, and it grows back in weeks. Roots work the same way: a cable shaves what it can reach, but root hairs in the joint stay alive and fill in again.

That is where jetting earns its place. The nozzle has jets that point back along the hose, so the water pressure pulls the hose forward while it scours the wall in a full circle. The technician pulls back slowly, and the line comes out close to its original bore.

Signs the line needs jetting, and when to call right away.

Any of these patterns suggests a coating problem more than a single blockage.

  • The same drain has been cabled more than once in the last year and keeps slowing down.
  • Water backs up in a basement floor drain or the lowest tub whenever the washer drains. Call right away if water is on the floor.
  • A previous cable job stopped at a bend or could not get past a certain distance.
  • Gurgling in a toilet when a sink runs, which points to a restricted shared line.
  • A kitchen line that smells of old grease even after the trap has been cleaned.
  • Tree roots grow near the sewer path, or a camera look has already shown fine roots in a joint.
  • A commercial or multi-unit kitchen where grease builds up on a schedule.

Why Orem lines pick up scale, grease and roots.

Most of Orem's houses went up between 1950 and 1980, so the sewer lateral serving them has often been in the ground for 40 to 70 years. Pipe that old has joints where root hairs enter looking for moisture, which is why root problems show up in older streets with mature trees. When roots are in the lateral that runs to the street, the job is closer to cleaning the whole sewer line than to clearing one fixture.

Orem's culinary water comes from wells, springs and Provo River water, and that mix can change by season and neighborhood. Mineral scale grows where hot water runs, which means the water heater side of a line and kitchen drains see it first. A camera look can show whether the narrowing is scale, grease or roots before jetting begins.

Our dispatch also covers Lindon and the rest of Utah County. The same logic applies there, because any lot with large trees and an older lateral can carry the same root problem.

How the jetting is done, step by step.

The plumber finds an access point first, usually a cleanout, which is a capped pipe in the yard, basement or crawlspace. If there is no cleanout, access may come from a pulled toilet or a roof vent. The hose and nozzle go in with a cable or a camera ahead of them when the line needs to be seen.

Pressure is set for the pipe material and condition. Typical jetting uses a few thousand pounds per square inch at the pump, though the plumber chooses a setting for the line, not a fixed number. Cast iron, clay, PVC and old cement-lined pipe react differently, and a fragile pipe may call for lower pressure or no jetting at all.

Debris ends up downstream in the city sewer main, and the plumber checks the flow afterward. If a sewer camera inspection is done after jetting, the pipe wall is clean enough for a reliable read of any cracks or offsets.

What affects the cost of hydro jetting.

Jetting uses heavier equipment than cabling, so the quote reflects the setup as well as the time.

Length and diameter of the line

A short 2 inch kitchen branch is quicker than a 4 inch lateral run of 60 feet or more.

Access point

A working cleanout speeds the job. If the plumber has to pull a toilet or cut in a new access, that adds time.

Condition of the pipe

Roots, scale and grease need different passes, and a heavily root-bound line takes more time.

Camera work

A before or after camera run adds time but shows what the water did and what the pipe needs.

Hour of the call

Where jetting stops: limits worth knowing.

Jetting cleans pipe. It does not fix a crack, a collapsed section or a pipe that has sagged and holds water. If a camera shows broken pipe, high pressure can push water out of the break, so the plumber may recommend a spot repair or a replacement instead.

Some fragile or very old pipe is not a good candidate. A plumber should say so rather than force a job. A camera cannot see through standing water, so a bellied line may need a pass of pumping first.

Jetting also does not replace a sump pump, a septic tank pump-out or a roof drain. Those are separate trades.
