Why Orem plumbing is aging all at once

Orem grew from 8,351 residents in 1950 to 52,399 in 1980. A large share of Orem's homes date from those post-war growth decades, so many still carry original supply and drain lines. Check your home's build year before assuming either way.

Source: U.S. Decennial Census counts for Orem, Utah, as tabulated on Wikipedia. Highlighted bars mark the 1960 to 1980 boom decades.

What the water and ground in Orem do to pipes.

According to the city's 2022 water conservation plan, Orem draws about 62 percent of its water from the Provo River, 28 percent from deep wells and 11 percent from springs. Because the sources differ, water chemistry can shift by season and by where a home sits, so ask the plumber what your fixtures and heater are seeing.

The same plan describes a leak detection program and a galvanized service line replacement program. If your home dates from the boom years, a galvanized line between the meter and the house is a real possibility, and a pressure drop or rusty water is the usual first sign.

Frost matters too. The city's design criteria list a frost line of 30 inches, so buried lines shallower than that are exposed to freeze damage, and Utah Geological Survey guidance notes that shallow groundwater can flood basements in low valley areas like Utah Valley. After the August 2024 storm overwhelmed storm drains in Orem, sump pumps and backwater protection became a bigger question for many homeowners.

Before you call, gather these.

A minute of preparation shortens the call and helps the plumber bring the right parts.

  • Your street address and the nearest cross street.
  • Where the water or backup is, and whether it is still running.
  • Whether you have shut off the main valve, and where it is.
  • The age and fuel type of your water heater if the problem involves hot water.
  • Photos of the damage or the model label on the equipment, if you can take them safely.
  • Any earlier repairs on the same line, which help the plumber guess the cause.
