Sewer backup in American Fork: who owns which pipe.

The city's Public Works pages explain that American Fork has been lining cracked sewer pipes from the inside to cut down on groundwater leaking into them. That work covers city mains. The pipe running from your house to the main is a different asset, and if several fixtures gurgle or the lowest drain overflows first, the blockage is often in that private line or in the main just past it.

Call the Sewer Division about the main (its number is on the city's Sewer page, along with an after-hours line) and ask where the city's responsibility ends. For the private line, sewer line cleaning with a cable machine or a high-pressure water jet clears a stoppage, and a camera shows whether the cause is grease, roots or a broken pipe.

Cracks are what let roots in. Where a camera finds a damaged section, the same inside-the-pipe lining idea the city uses on its mains may be an option, and sewer line repair options such as lining or replacement depend on the pipe material, depth and what is planted above it. Lining does not suit every pipe, which is why a camera inspection comes before any quote.

Sewage in the house right now: urgent steps.

Stop all water use and do not flush. Keep children and pets away from standing water, and do not mix drain chemicals, since they can splash back on you and damage the pipe a plumber is about to clear.

If sewage is near outlets, appliances or the panel, keep people away from wet electrical areas, and shut power off at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. A wet yard: drinking-water leak or pressurized irrigation?.

American Fork has two water systems at the curb. The pressurized irrigation system finished construction in 2010, delivers untreated water for outdoor use only, and runs from April 15 to October 15 each year. Connecting to it is optional, and the city has installed meters on irrigation connections, with metered usage rates approved to take effect January 1, 2027.

A leak on the irrigation side wastes untreated water that will soon be billed by use, while a leak on the culinary side shows on the culinary meter. Shut off every fixture and watch that meter's leak indicator. If it moves, a plumber has a leak to find. Sprinkler heads, zone valves and drip lines are yard irrigation work that plumbing dispatch may not cover, so ask when you call whether the repair fits.

Basement water and sump pumps near the American Fork River.

Utah Geological Survey guidance on shallow groundwater says it is generally within 10 feet of the surface, is more likely in low-lying valley areas and near rivers, streams and lakes, and can flood basements. American Fork has a river running through it and Utah Lake to the south, so a basement here can see groundwater without any plumbing failure at all. The same guidance advises inspecting sump pumps annually, in winter, before the problem season.

A plumber can test a pump, replace a failed one, fix a stuck check valve and trace a frozen or blocked discharge. That is the scope of sump pump repair, and it is not foundation waterproofing or excavation work. Standard homeowners insurance typically does not cover groundwater flooding, so read your own policy before assuming a claim.

Hot water problems in American Fork homes.

Water heaters fail in two ways. A leak from the tank body usually means the tank has corroded and replacement is the honest answer, while a drip at a fitting or the relief valve can sometimes be repaired. No hot water with no leak points to an element, a thermostat or a gas control, all of which a plumber can test on the spot.

If the unit is gas and you smell gas, leave the building immediately and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call us once you are outside and safe. Emergency water heater calls are handled at any hour, with same-day appointments when available for non-urgent work.

Before we arrive in American Fork: four things to do.

Small steps before the plumber arrives limit damage and speed the diagnosis.

Stop the source

Close the main valve for a pressure-side leak, or stop all water use for a sewer backup. Leave the meter alone.

Document the damage

Photograph the water line on walls and floors before cleanup. Sudden, accidental damage is what homeowners policies usually cover, while slow leaks, wear and groundwater flooding usually are not, so read your own policy.

Find the cleanout

If you know where the sewer cleanout, water heater or sump pit is, clear a path to it. Tell the dispatcher where it is on the call.

Ask the neighbors

If the street is also without water, report it to the city. If only your house is affected, tell the dispatcher so the plumber starts at the meter and the main valve.
