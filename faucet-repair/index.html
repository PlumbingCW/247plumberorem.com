Most fixture trouble starts as a drip nobody bothered with. The part that wears out is small, usually a cartridge, a seat and spring, a ceramic disc or a rubber washer, but the damage it causes can run through a cabinet floor. Here is how those failures are sorted and what a visit involves.

Where the water shows up tells you which part has failed. This table covers the sink, tub, shower and outdoor fixtures most Orem homes have.

A tub or shower that fills with water around your ankles is a drain issue, not a faucet one. The same logic applies to a toilet that runs or leaks, which is handled under toilet repair.

How cartridge, disc and compression faucets differ.

Compression faucets use a rubber washer pressed on a seat, and the washer wears from every turn. Cartridge faucets slide a brass or plastic stem inside the body. Ceramic disc types use two polished plates that shear against each other. Each style fails in its own way, so the plumber identifies it before ordering parts.

Single-handle kitchen faucets often hold a cartridge or a ball assembly with rubber seats and springs. The kitchen sink also hosts the disposal and dishwasher hookup, and a leak below the sink may actually belong to the garbage disposal. Pull-down sprayers add a hose with a weight and a quick-connect fitting that can leak where it joins the faucet shank. Bathroom widespread sets have separate hot and cold valves that may be repaired one at a time.

Repair is usually cheaper than replacement when the body is sound and parts are available. A pitted chrome finish, a cracked spout or a model no longer supported by the maker tips the choice toward replacing the fixture. If the faucet is old and the supply stops are corroded, the two are often done together.

Broken shut-off valve or spraying fixture: what to do right now.

A snapped supply valve is the one case where a small fixture floods a house. Close the main shutoff, usually where the water line enters the home near the water heater or a garage wall, then open a low faucet to drain the pressure. Move items out of the cabinet and keep people away from wet electrical areas.

Do not crank a stuck angle stop with pliers. A valve that is seized or corroded can shear off the stem and leave the line open. If the main will not close, that is a faster call, and nights and weekends get the same 24/7 dispatch.

A slow drip from a spout or a weeping handle is not an emergency. Place a bowl under it, close the angle stop, and ask for a same-day appointment when available instead of paying for an after-hours visit.

Orem water, old stops and frozen spigots.

Orem draws on wells, city-owned springs and treated Provo River water, so mineral chemistry can vary by season and by where a home sits. Scale that clogs an aerator or locks up a cartridge is common where water is heated and flows through small passages. If a faucet slows down a few months after a repair, ask the plumber to check the aerator and the cartridge seats for deposits.

Spigots that are not frost-proof, or hoses left connected over winter, can freeze inside the wall and crack the body. Even a frost-proof bib can freeze if a hose stays on it, because the hose traps water in the barrel.

The leak only shows when you turn the water back on in spring, sometimes as a stain inside the wall that leak detection with acoustic and thermal tools can trace. Closing the interior valve and draining the spigot before a hard freeze avoids it.

If your home dates from the postwar decades, the angle stops and tub valves may be original. Replacing the stop when a faucet is repaired is cheap insurance against the next midnight failure.

What a faucet repair visit looks like.

The sequence is the same for a kitchen mixer, a bath valve or a laundry connection.

  1. Isolate the water

    The plumber closes the fixture stop, or the main shutoff if the stops are bad, and opens the faucet to confirm flow has stopped.

  2. Identify the style

    The handle, escutcheon and spout are removed to reveal the cartridge, disc or washer so the exact part number is known.

  3. Replace the failed parts

    Seats, O-rings, springs, cartridges or the whole valve body are swapped, and supply lines or stops are replaced if they are weak.

  4. Test under pressure

    Water is restored slowly, every joint is checked with dry paper, and hot and cold mixing is confirmed before the cabinet is reloaded.

Before you phone about a leaking fixture.

  • Close the angle stops under the sink and note whether the leak stops, which separates a faucet fault from a supply fault.
  • Take a photo of the faucet brand mark, usually on the base or under the handle, so the right cartridge is quoted.
  • Check cabinet floors and drywall for soft spots or staining and photograph them for any insurance claim.
  • Test whether hot or cold only is dripping, since that narrows the cartridge or valve involved.
  • Clear the cabinet and lay a towel under the pipes so the plumber can reach the stops.
  • Note whether a leaking shower or tub shares a wall with a ceiling below, and look there for stains.
