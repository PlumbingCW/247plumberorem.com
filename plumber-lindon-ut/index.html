Lindon emergency: what to do first.

For a sewage backup, stop all water use and plug floor drains and tubs. Lindon's sewer backup handout says to ask the neighbors, because a blockage in the city main is cleared by Public Works (801-796-7954) and one in your own lateral is the owner's job. If the neighbors are fine, call us.

Who owns which pipe in Lindon.

Lindon's retail water line disclosure puts the city in charge of the drinking water line up to and including the meter, and the owner in charge from the meter to the house. A wet patch between the meter and the foundation is therefore your repair, usually a water service line repair. A leak on the street side of the meter belongs to Public Works, so check the meter lid before you pay for a dig.

The sewer lateral works the same way. The city's notice says repairs to the pipe from the house to the main are the owner's, while the city clears blockages in its own main. Duties may differ for HOA and private-road homes. The irrigation line splits at the stop-and-waste valve or secondary meter, with the owner responsible from that point into the yard.

Secondary water: a second pipe system that runs from April to October.

Lindon's pressurized irrigation system, installed about 1993, serves most residential areas. The city starts filling it in mid-April, water is usually available around May 1, and the canal supply stops around October 15. The water is screened but not filtered, so it is never for drinking and never to be cross-connected. After the fall drain-down, a wet patch in the yard is more likely a drinking water or drain problem than irrigation.

The city tells residents to open the valve slowly in spring and check for leaks, since lines can freeze over winter. A fountain at the box in May is usually a freeze break, and a leak detection visit separates it from a culinary leak by watching whether the house meter moves.

Sump pumps, sewers and storm drains are three separate systems.

Lindon's storm drains are a separate system, not connected to the sewer. The city says it is illegal to connect a sump pump to the sanitary lateral, because the extra flow can back sewage into basements. A sump pump repair should therefore end with a discharge line that goes to the yard or the storm system.

Lindon built its sewer collection system in 1974 to 1978, and the flow ends at the Orem wastewater treatment plant. A lateral from that era can have cracked joints or roots. When a line backs up twice, a sewer camera inspection shows the pipe instead of clearing it blind.

Digging, permits and licenses in Lindon.

Statewide, not just in Lindon, Blue Stakes of Utah (811) needs at least three business days of notice before excavation. Utility owners mark only their own lines, so your private water or sewer line may not be marked and the plumber should locate it first.

Lindon's encroachment permit for right-of-way work asks the contractor for utility maps, a traffic control plan, a state contractor license and liability insurance, so a repair on the street side of the meter is city territory. For work on your own property, the Lindon Building Department at 801-785-7687 can say which permits apply, since rules vary by job.

Before the plumber reaches your Lindon property.

Four short tasks speed up an overnight visit.

Read the meter

Close every tap and watch the dial. A moving dial with a dry house points to a leak between meter and house.

Find the cleanout

A capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement is the sewer cleanout. Clear the area around it.

Check your neighbors

If the next house has the same backup, the blockage may be in the city main.

Protect the floor

Lift rugs and electronics, photograph the damage and keep people from wet outlets.
